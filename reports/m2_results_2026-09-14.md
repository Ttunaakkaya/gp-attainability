# M2 — recorded timed Bellman/DP integration

14 September 2026. Independent implementation and development evidence; **not**
ECC2025 reproduction, M6 held-out evaluation, a belief-MDP optimum, or a physical
safety/attainability certificate.

## Outcome and repeatability

All **36 policy runs in 10 comparisons completed**. The real received-data GP →
timed Bellman candidates → joint covariance selection → QP → new measurement loop
works. The new periodic DP policy is B3; the existing adaptive greedy is B2.
M2 establishes that hierarchy and its auditable records, **not that B3 is superior**.
In the eight 90 s development conditions, B3 had higher RMSE in five and lower RMSE
in three; its mean RMSE was slightly higher and its mean latent variance was higher.
The longer pilot also favored B2 on both metrics. All outcomes are retained.

## Protocol and implementation boundary

The first two runs are a 300 s, four-robot, combined-disturbance pilot at seed 7,
using B2 and B3. The matrix is `2/4 robots × nominal/combined × seeds 7/19 ×
sweep/nominal greedy/B2/B3`, with 90 simulated seconds per policy: 32 runs.
Two final B2/B3 runs use a 17 s mission to exercise an unsampled terminal tail.

Every policy uses the same 60 × 40 m field, exact GP, 0.5 s control interval,
5 s measurement clock, 2 m/s speed bound, 2 m separation and M1 holonomic QP.
Combined conditions use 30% loss and 0.35 m/s command-perturbation standard
deviation. Noise/loss keys, starts, deadlines and sensor counts are paired;
measurement values and locations depend on each policy's actual execution.
Disturbances enter **before** the constraint controller, not after its command.

B3 searches a 9 × 7 coarse grid, at most four future measurement epochs, and
two robot orders for two robots or four orders for four robots. There is also an
explicit nominal hold candidate. The backward Bellman surrogate freezes normalized
single-sample variance-reduction rewards and uses travel weight 0.01. Earlier
robots' candidate sample locations condition later robots' covariance rewards.
Final selection minimizes **exact joint terminal mean latent variance**; travel
only breaks ties within `1e-10 × signal_variance`. Every prefix forecast comes
from that one selected chronological multi-robot plan.

Frozen rewards can favor revisiting cells and do not solve the full belief-MDP.
The grid/action set and synchronized arrival also affect traveled distance.
Short greedy and previous-plan-retention candidates are not implemented here.
Nominal continuous straight-segment geometry is screened, but there is **no QP
candidate rollout**, SOGP backend, M4 recovery logic or USV-heading DP. Forecasts
assume nominal sample locations and successful receipt, not guaranteed attainment.

was **17.762 s**. The full batch, including serialization and independent audits,

Per-plan statistics pool actual `planning_wall_s` values, not per-run quantiles.

comparison validation. Desktop/OS scheduling affects these observed wall times.
No other project test/benchmark was deliberately run alongside the batch, but
these are not isolated microbenchmarks, worst-case bounds or real-time guarantees.
Planner latency is not deducted from simulated mission time.

SciPy 1.17.1, OSQP 1.1.3 and PyArrow 23.0.1; each bundle has its own metadata.

For two robots, combined seed 7, B3 reduced mean variance (0.148627 versus
0.164874) while worsening RMSE. For four robots, combined seed 19, it improved
RMSE while increasing variance (0.063050 versus 0.051917). Thus variance and
field accuracy remain separate quantities, not interchangeable success measures.

The longer pilot is also negative evidence: B3 RMSE **0.097464** versus B2
**0.079178**, mean variance **0.013351** versus **0.007941**, and fleet distance
**1160.52 m** versus **1965.07 m**; each received 179 of 244 attempted measurements.
Shorter travel is not proof of energy efficiency, better mapping, or task rescue.
No statistical significance or general superiority claim follows from this set.
