# Hatanaka Lab fit: input to the project interview

Checked: 18 September 2026. Scope: a bounded fit check for GP Attainability, not a novelty review or an admissions assessment. User priority: a concrete, defensible result for Professor Takeshi Hatanaka; portfolio value is secondary. Method choices remain open, and repeated 6–8+ hour experiments are a concern.

## What the primary sources support

- **Lab direction.** The faculty page identifies Hatanaka as a professor at Institute of Science Tokyo and describes distributed decision-making, networked control, optimality and robustness, with applications including smart agriculture and smart ocean. It also explicitly describes practical implementation of networked robotics. This establishes thematic relevance, not a preference for any particular applicant or proposal. [Official faculty page](https://hatanakalab.wixsite.com/website/faculty)
- **Environmental sampling with GPs.** The official 2025 list records the ECC paper by Suenaga, Hanif, Uto and Hatanaka on hierarchical multi-robot environmental sampling through online GP, and an undergraduate study on sampling through online sparse GP. [Official 2025 publications](https://hatanakalab.wixsite.com/website/%E8%A4%87%E8%A3%BD-2024)
- **An inspected first-party explanation.** Coauthor Hanif's page describes discrete sensing, a sparse online GP, a long-horizon planner and constrained QPs. It reports local-control deadlock and distinguishes posterior-variance progress from mean-squared reconstruction error. It also reports eventual violation of the specified variance-decay constraint in its simulations. These are statements in the coauthor's account; this check did not independently audit the paper's equations, assumptions, or guarantees. [Coauthor's ECC 2025 article page](https://mhd-hanif.github.io/publication/2025-paper-suenaga-et-al)
- **Constrained vessel control.** The 2025 list records a TCST article on constraint-driven multi-USV coverage and a book chapter combining vessel safety, CBF, MPC and path planning. These titles support the relevance of control and optimization expertise; their algorithms were not inspected here. [Official 2025 publications](https://hatanakalab.wixsite.com/website/%E8%A4%87%E8%A3%BD-2024)
- **Continued activity.** The main publication page lists a 2026 multi-USV monitoring benchmark chapter, a thesis on vessel control integrating constraints and MPC, and an undergraduate project progressing multi-drone scalar-field estimation toward field experiments. It separately labels work such as stochastic MPC as re-submitted and other entries as submitted or forthcoming; those statuses must not be collapsed into published results. [Official current publications](https://hatanakalab.wixsite.com/website/publication)
- **Concrete evaluation entry point.** The lab-owned benchmark README specifies Ubuntu 22.04, ROS 2 Humble and Python 3.10. Its documented launch parameter permits 1–4 agents and records output to rosbags. No runtime, GPU requirement, scientific metric, GP implementation or controller compatibility was verified from this README. [Multi-USV-Benchmark](https://github.com/htnk-lab/Multi-USV-Benchmark)

## Three plausible fits — inference, not established contribution claims

1. **Diagnose and recover a time-limited mapping mission.** Test whether a robot team can identify likely failure to reach a declared mapping objective early enough to improve the outcome through a simple response. This follows naturally from the environmental-sampling theme and uses the project's existing motivation. Prediction quality, intervention benefit and map error must be evaluated separately. Novelty remains unverified.
2. **Make sampling plans executable under physical and safety constraints.** Focus on the measured effect of travel, turning, safety filtering and timing on information collection. This offers a direct connection to the user's reported mechatronics, GP-MPC, optimization and safety-filter background. A controlled, narrow comparison would be more interpretable than adding all these ingredients at once.
3. **Demonstrate transfer to a small lab-relevant vessel benchmark.** A 2–4 agent experiment could provide concrete evidence of engineering competence. The benchmark's ROS/Linux setup is an additional integration task, so it is a candidate later validation stage rather than a prerequisite for settling the scientific question.

## Decisions still needed from the user

- Which capability should the result primarily demonstrate: mission diagnosis/recovery, constrained planning/control, or robust environmental estimation?
- Which parts of the reported background can the user already implement and explain independently, and which need learning time?
- What is the first presentation/contact deadline, and what CPU, RAM, operating-system/WSL access and experiment runtime budget are actually available?
- Is a rigorous simulation result sufficient for that first milestone, with benchmark integration later, or is vessel/ROS evidence essential from the start?
